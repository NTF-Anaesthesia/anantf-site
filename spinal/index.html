    Spinal anaesthesia

    Do it safely at the bedside, fix it when it goes wrong, and know it for the exam.

    Created by Dr Koh Wenjun and Dr Chew Shi Hao, NTF Anaesthesia

      I need to…

      • Prepare and positionChecks, set-up, sitting or lateral
      • Choose drug and doseDrug, baricity and label dose ranges
      • Anticoagulant timingWhen is a spinal safe, and the next dose
      • Dry tap or can’t find the spaceNo CSF: step-by-step
      • HypotensionLow BP or slow heart rate after the block
      • Scoliotic or elderly spineDifficult back: approach and ultrasound

      • Ward cardOne page to use at the bedside
      • Self-quizTest yourself
      • FRCA exam knowledgeCaesarean and infant spinals, trials, viva answers

      Chapters

      1. Ward cardThe short version: steps, doses and red flags on one page. Prints cleanly.
      2. Anatomy & physiologySpine, cord, meninges, dermatomes and what the block does to the body.
      3. PharmacologyLocal anaesthetics, baricity, adjuvants and vasopressors.
      4. TechniquePreparation, positioning, the step-by-step spinal, drugs and doses, special patients.
      5. Difficult backs & ultrasoundScoliosis, elderly and obese spines; ultrasound-assisted neuraxial.
      6. TroubleshootingDecision trees: dry tap, bloody tap, paraesthesia, failed or patchy block, hypotension.
      7. Complications & aftercareHeadache, nerve injury, haematoma, infection, high block, LAST and follow-up.
      8. Exam (FRCA) & quizExam-level knowledge, viva points and a self-quiz.
      9. ReferencesGuidelines cited on this page.
