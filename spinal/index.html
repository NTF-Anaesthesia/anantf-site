Spinal in 60 seconds

A safe single-shot spinal, from consent to block check. Details follow in the sections below.

  1. Consent. Explain the benefits, the common effects and the rare serious risks. Offer the alternative. Confirm the side and site.
  2. Bleeding and infection check. Anticoagulants, platelets and clotting, and any sepsis or skin infection near the site.
  3. Brief your senior. Say what you plan to do, and who is nearby if it goes wrong.
  4. Set up. Monitors on, working IV, oxygen, airway kit and a vasopressor ready. Have a trained assistant.
  5. Check the drug. Read the ampoule label, with a second person. Right drug, right dose, in date. Use neuraxial-only connectors.
  6. Position. Sitting or lateral, back arched. Choose an interspace well below the end of the cord, usually L3/4 or L4/5.
  7. Aseptic technique. Hands, mask, sterile gloves, skin prep left to dry fully, sterile drape.
  8. Needle in slowly. Local anaesthetic to the skin. Advance in small steps and stop to check for CSF each time.
  9. Inject only with free CSF. Attach, check CSF flows, inject slowly. Never inject against resistance or into pain.
  10. Lie back and check. Position as planned, then BP and heart rate every few minutes. Test the sensory level and leg movement before surgery. Write it down.

Stop and get help if

  • Pain or persistent paraesthesia when the needle goes in or on injection. Don’t inject. Withdraw.
  • Blood-stained CSF that doesn’t clear, or you aren’t sure the clotting is safe.
  • Falling blood pressure, slow heart rate, breathlessness or drowsiness after the injection. Call for help, give oxygen and treat early.
