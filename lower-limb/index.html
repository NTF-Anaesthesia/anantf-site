Rapid fire

Eight things to know before any lower-limb block

  1. Femoral nerve = quadriceps. A femoral or fascia iliaca block weakens the leg: check strength before the first stand. Choose PENG or an adductor canal block when the patient must walk.
  2. Fascia iliaca does not reliably block the obturator nerve, whatever the volume.
  3. Popliteal misses the saphenous strip: the medial leg and the medial malleolus. Add a saphenous block.
  4. An ankle block numbs the foot, not the ankle joint: the joint branches leave above the malleoli.
  5. Ask where the cuff goes. Ankle cuff: ankle block. Calf cuff: popliteal + saphenous. Thigh cuff: spinal or general anaesthesia.
  6. A spinal does not stop the obturator jerk. Block the obturator nerve before the resection.
  7. A numb foot can’t warn you: float the heel, pad the fibular neck, and think compartment syndrome when new pain breaks through.
  8. Count every milligram, the surgeon’s infiltration included. A common ceiling for ropivacaine is about 3 mg/kg (lean body weight), lower in the frail.

What each block costs in muscle

PENG
Usually none. Quadriceps weakness if fluid reaches the femoral nerve.
LFCN
None. Purely sensory.
Fascia iliaca, femoral
Quadriceps. The knee buckles: falls.
Adductor canal
Largely spared. Vastus medialis if too proximal or too much volume.
iPACK, genicular
None. iPACK can spread to the tibial or common peroneal nerve.
Obturator
Hip adductors.
Sciatic, popliteal
Foot drop, no push-off; the hamstrings too with the subgluteal level.
Ankle
Intrinsic foot muscles only. Walks on the heel.

Doses are ropivacaine for an adult, as used on the block pages. They are starting points: adjust for weight, age, frailty and every other local anaesthetic the patient receives.

Local anaesthetic systemic toxicity. Lower-limb combinations use large volumes, often in frail or sick patients: two or three blocks plus the surgeon’s infiltration can pass the maximum dose. Add up the milligrams before you start, inject in small aliquots with aspiration, and know where the lipid emulsion is.
