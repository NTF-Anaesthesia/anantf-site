5. Describe the physiological effects of a high-flow nasal cannula device (HFNC) e.g.
OptiflowTM or AirvoTM. (30%). Briefly list the indications, and justify its use in an adult
endoscopy suite (70%).
(Modified from ANZCA 2018A)

Examiner Report




(a) Physiological effects of HFNC include
Respi
• Allows for High FiO2
       o O2 reservoir by washing out nitrogen in FRC and dead spaces
       o Apnoeic oxygenation which prolongs apnoeic time
       o May result in resorption atelectasis
• Passive ventilation by means of bulk flow/ Pendelluft effect
       o Improves carbon dioxide clearance
• Warming and humification of airways
       o Improves secretion clearance and ciliary function
• CPAP effect from high air flows
       o Improved V/Q ratio from alveolar recruitment and decreased atelectasis.
       o Airway splinting effect maintains airway patency, and reduces work of breathing
       o Risk of barotrauma if excessively high airway pressures or in emphysematous lungs

CVS
• Afterload may improve from reduced pulmonary congestion/ oedema from CPAP
• Venous return/ preload may worsen from positive intra-thoracic pressures generated
   from CPAP
• Effect on cardiac output is variable, depending on the interplay between afterload vs
   preload effects

GI
• Aerophagia from excessive positive airway pressure, especially in patients with GERD/
   reduced lower oesophageal sphincter tone.
• Risk of aspiration secondary to regurgitation from aerophagia

(b) Indications for HFNC
• Type 1 Respiratory Failure
        o Cardiogenic pulmonary oedema
        o Non-cardiogenic T1RF
• Pre-oxygenation/ apnoeic oxygenation for
        o High risk group (morbidly obese patient)
        o Awake fibre-optic intubation
        o Bronchoscopic procedures
•   Transitioning from invasive ventilation in ICU
•   Post-extubation NIV to reduce atelectasis/ post-op hypnoxemia for high risk patients
    (cardiac, major abdominal surgery, OSA)

(c) Justify its use in adult endoscopy suite
HFNC provides high FiO2, passive ventilation, CPAP, and warm and humidified gases in a
compact, portable package, which is useful in a remote location with confined spaces such as
an endoscopy suite.

For procedures done under sedation/monitored anaesthetic care, HFNC is useful because it:
• Allows for delivery of higher FiO2 without interfering with procedure (compared to non-
    rebreather mask NRM)
• Maintains airway patency during sedation/ MAC
• Allows for hands-free airway management
• Increases margin of safety for desaturation/ respiratory compromise in complex patient
    group
• Improves patient comfort and tolerance via delivery of warm, humified gases

For procedures done under GA with intubation, HFNC is useful because it:
• Facilitates pre-oxygenation, and prolongs apnoeic time prior to intubation. This is
    important in a remote location where help may be difficult to get.
• Allows for post-extubation NIV in complex patient group, and reduces re-intubation rate
    in recovery room post-procedure

Caution with HFNC use in the endoscopy room for
• Patients at high risk of aspiration/reflux, which could be the indication for scopes.
• Patients with severe cardio-respiratory disease. It would be safer to have these patients
   done in major OT with appropriate facility and staff support
• If staff are inexperienced or unfamiliar with its use
• May lead to false sense of security as SpO2 may appear to be satisfactory even though
   patient is deteriorating
