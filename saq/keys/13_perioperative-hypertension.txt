A patient presents on the day of surgery with a blood pressure of 180/110mmHg. Justify your
decision to proceed with or postpone surgery for this patient.

Sample Answer

The stem provides insufficient information about the patient and nature of surgery. In such a situation,
my decision would be to postpone surgery based on the following assumptions:
(1) This is an elective surgery.
(2) Blood pressure readings were repeated and verified to be non-spurious.

Justifications
My decision to postpone surgery is supported by the 2016 joint guidelines by AABGI and British
Hypertension Society, which recommends a threshold of BP <180/110 mmHg before proceeding with
elective surgery.

In addition, the elevated blood pressure of 180/110 mmHg in this patient could represent several
scenarios where it would not be medically appropriate to proceed:
1. Hypertensive Urgency/ Emergency
        - Priority would be to rule out and treat potential end-organ injuries such as stroke and AMI,
             which would increase peri-op mortality and morbidity if left untreated.
2. Poorly controlled Hypertension
        - More labile haemodynamic profile, leading to greater fluctuations in BP and HR and
             increased risk of intra-op hypertension.
        - Increased sympathetic response to intra-op stimulus, which can lead to high BP and
             increased bleeding.
        - Associated with increased risk of atherosclerosis in major end organs – increasing risk of
             stroke, CAD, CKD, PVD. These co-morbidities should be worked up and optimised prior to
             surgery.
3. Newly diagnosed Hypertension
        - These patients should start treatment and attain satisfactory BP control prior to surgery
             to reduce their risk of MACE events.
        - Need to exclude secondary causes for hypertension if patient is young.

Given that surgery postponement comes with financial/ social/ psychological costs to hospital and
patient, I will discuss with the surgeon to get his input. I will also explain and counsel my patient so
that he may understand the rationale for surgery postponement .

Caveats
However, there are specific situations where benefits outweigh risks, and I may proceed with surgery
after counselling for elevated MACE risks based on patient/ surgical risk profiles. These include:
1. Emergency (life/limb/eye-threatening conditions) or Time-sensitive (malignancy/ severe infection
    requiring urgent source control) surgeries
2. Surgeries for BP management (e.g. adrenalectomy for pheochromocytoma, caesarean section for
    parturient with pre-eclampsia)
3. Persistently high blood pressure despite compliance to medical treatment and optimization by
    medical team
4. White coat hypertension (normal home BP monitoring, with secondary causes of hypertension
    ruled out)
