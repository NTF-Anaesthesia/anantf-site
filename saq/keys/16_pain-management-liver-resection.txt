ANZCA 2017 List the methods of analgesia that can be used for patients undergoing open
liver resection surgery. (30%). Justify your perioperative pain management strategy. (70%)

Sample Answer (for 2nd part of question)
Pre-op:
I would review patient in clinic for pre-op assessment and counselling, which includes
- Assessment of underlying liver disease and co-morbidities (e.g. renal impairment and
    coagulopathy)
- Optimisation of existing painful conditions, if any, which may include opioids for cancer-
    related pain.
        o Prescribed analgesics by primary oncologist/ surgeon should be continued during
           the peri-op period.
- Counselling of anaesthesia/ analgesia plan
I would also make use of this opportunity to allay patient’s concerns and manage patient’s
expectations, which could possibly improve patient’s satisfaction and self-reported pain
scores.

Intra-op:
I would employ an opioid-sparing, multimodal pain management strategy to cover for both
somatic (open resection) and visceral (visceral tissue manipulation/ dissection/ trauma) pain.
Good intra-op analgesia facilitates maintenance of stable haemodynamics during the surgery,
and smooth emergence from anaesthesia at the end of surgery.
- Basic analgesia
       o I would administer IV Paracetamol 1g and IV Parecoxib 40mg at the start of the
           surgery if there are no contra-indications (severe hepatic impairment, platelet
           dysfunction, hepato-renal syndrome, ischemic heart disease).
       o This is in accordance to ESRA PROSPECT recommendation for analgesia for open
           liver resection.
- Adjunct analgesia
       o In line with an opioid sparing technique, I would employ the use of adjunct
           analgesia intra-op as there are supporting evidence from RCT studies for open
           abdominal surgery, showing reduction in opioid usage.
               § IV Magnesium Sulphate 30 mg/kg loading dose, followed by 10 mg/kg/h
               § IV Dexmedetomidine 0.6 mcg/kg loading dose, followed by 0.2 mcg/kg/h
               § IV Ketamine 300 mcg/kg loading dose, followed by 100 mcg/kg/h
       o I would stop the infusions 30-40min before the end of the surgery (1) to minimise
           sedation during emergence and (2) to facilitate NMBA reversal (as MgSO4
           potentiates NMB).
- Opioids
       o TCI Remifentanil, an ultra-short acting opioids with organ-independent
           metabolism may be titrated intra-op to supplement my basic/ adjunct analgesia,
           especially if patient was already on opioids prior to surgery.
- Regional analgesia
       o My preference would be for bilateral subcostal TAP catheters, to be inserted at
           the end of surgery. Single bolus 0.2% Ropivacaine 30ml per side, followed by
           running 5-7ml/h on each side.
       o There is robust evidence for use of TAP block catheter to reduce pain score and
         post-op opioid consumption, and is included in ESRA PROSPECT recommendations
         for open liver surgery.
       o While thoracic epidural has been demonstrated to be superior to subcostal TAP
         block/catheter in terms of analgesia, I would avoid an epidural due to potential
         concerns of (1) post-resection coagulopathy resulting in increased risk of epidural
         haematoma, (2) post-op hypotension requiring vasopressor/ fluid resuscitation,
         which may delay recovery and early mobilisation.

Post-op:
My post-op pain management goals would be to provide adequate analgesia to (1) enable
effective deep breathing/chest physiotherapy (to prevent post-op atelectasis/pneumonia),
(2) early mobilisation (reducing DVT/PE risk), while (3) avoiding complications associated with
opioid use (e.g. respiratory depression, nausea/vomiting, drowsiness, constipation)
- Basic analgesia
        o Regular Paracetamol and COX-II inhibitors (Etoricoxib/ Parecoxib) should be
           scheduled for the next 5 days.
        o Dose adjustment of Paracetamol in consultation with surgeon may be required if
           there is impaired liver function post-resection. Monitoring of post-op renal
           function while on COX-II inhibitors should also be done.
- Regional analgesia
        o I would continue to run 0.2% Ropivacaine 5-7ml/h per side via subcostal TAP
           catheters for the next 3-5 days. This covers the somatic pain (from surgical
           incision), but does not provide analgesia from visceral pain.
- Opioids
        o In institution with an established pain service for post-op monitoring, my
           preference is for intrathecal morphine 300-400 mcg (administered pre-induction)
           to cover for visceral pain. This reduces the total amount of systemic opioids post-
           op, improves patient satisfaction, and pain score for 24-30 hours.
        o I would minimise the use of systemic opioids, and limit its usage for breakthrough
           pain. PCA fentanyl would be my preferred choice for breakthrough pain as it has
           fast onset with no active metabolites.
- No role for adjunct analgesia (gabapentinoids, dexamethasone) in the post-op period
    based on current evidence.
