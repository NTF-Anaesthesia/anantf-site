A 40 year old man with hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy (HOCM) presents for
elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy.
    (a) Describe the principles of intraoperative haemodynamic management for this
        patient. (40%)
    (b) How would you manage hypotension post induction of general anaesthesia in this
        patient? (60%)
(Modified from ANZCA 2010A)

Examiner Report
Sample Answer
(a)
Principles of intra-operative haemodynamic management would be dependent on:
Severity of HOCM
• AICD in-situ (suggesting risk of malignant arrhythmias)
• Past and current treatment, including B-blockers
• ECG: LVH, arrhythmias
• 2DE findings: Assess biventricular contractility, degree of LVOT obstruction, presence of
    Systolic Anterior Motion (SAM), diastolic dysfunction, volume status
Effect of surgery on HOCM
• Pneumoperitoneum (Increased IAP reducing venous return/ preload/ vagal stimulation)
• CO2 absorption (may cause tachycardia/arrhythmias)
• Reverse Trendelenburg position (decrease preload)

Principles of intra-operative haemodynamic management for this patient would include:
Avoid worsening dynamic LVOT obstruction
• Use of TEE assessment of biventricular contractility
• Keeping the LV well-filled by maintaining
        o High-normal preload
                § Adequate IV fluid loading before Reverse Trendelenburg
                    position/pneumoperitoneum
                § Goal directed fluid therapy using cardiac output monitor (Flotrac)
        o High-normal afterload/ systemic vascular resistance
                § Vasopressors (Phenylephrine, Vasopressin) to maintain BP during
                    induction
                § Avoid histamine releasing drugs (Rocuronium over Atracurium, Oxycodone
                    over Morphine)
• Maintain low-normal contractility
• Maintain low-normal heart rate
Optimise coronary perfusion and myocardial O2 supply
• Check and treat anaemia
• Titrate FiO2/PEEP to keep SpO2>95%
• Maintain diastolic BP (high-normal afterload), diastolic time (slow HR) and filling to
    maintain coronary perfusion pressure
Avoid surges in myocardial O2 demand
• Continue beta-blockers perioperatively if patient is already on them
• Avoid sympathetic surges
        o Blunt laryngoscopy response with esmolol/lignocaine/alfentanil
        o Adequate depth of anaesthesia and analgesia
        o Titrate ventilation to aim ETCO2 35-40 mmHg (avoid hypercarbia)
        o Smooth extubation (on remifentanil)
• Treat tachyarrhythmias aggressively
Maintain sinus rhythm, with standby measures for malignant arrhythmias
• Check and correct electrolyte abnormalities/acidosis
• Ensure normothermia (temp probe monitoring, active warming devices)
•   Peri-op AICD management (if present), defibrillation pads, resus drugs drawn, crash cart
    on standby

(b)
I would manage post-induction hypotension based on severity of clinical problem. If
hypotension is profound and potentially life-threatening, I would declare crisis situation,
inform surgeon, and call for help (including defib pads and crash cart).

Possible causes include:
Patient-related       Worsening dynamic LVOT obstruction
                      Cardiac events (ischaemia, arrhythmia)
Anaesthesia-related Drop in SVR from induction agent
                      Anaphylaxis
                      Tension pneumothorax
Surgical-related      Pneumoperitoneum (if initiated)
                      Bleeding (if surgery has started)

I would simultaneously resuscitate patient according to ABC principles, and assess for other
possible causes of hypotension. In this patient with history of HOCM, I would be most
concerned about worsening dynamic LVOT obstruction.
Airway        Check and ensure correct ETT placement and patency
Breathing     Give 100% FiO2. Auscultate to exclude tension pneumothorax (absent breath
              sound, tracheal deviation, hyperresonance to percussion) , anaphylaxis
              (wheeze), pulmonary oedema (creps)
Circulation   Feel for pulse. Check other haemodynamic parameters (HR, SpO2, ETCO2) for
              abnormalities. Check ECG for ischaemic changes and arrhythmias
              Vasopressors (Phenylephrine) to improve BP/afterload
              Run IV crystalloids fast via 2x large bore IV cannula
              Stop/ cut down on anaesthetic agents
              Release pneumoperitoneum and level patient (if surgery has started)

Subsequent treatment should be directed at the cause of hypotension. Specific treatment for
worsening dynamic LVOT obstruction include:
• Increase afterload with direct a-agonist (phenylephrine)
• Increase preload by fluid resuscitation with IV crystalloids
• B-blocker to slow down HR and reduce contractility
• Treat arrhythmias aggressively (correct electrolytes, cardioversion)
• Treat causes of sympathetic stimulation (pain, hypercarbia) by optimising depth of
   anaesthesia/ analgesia and minute ventilation
• TEE to evaluate biventricular contractility, degree of LVOT obstruction, presence of SAM,
   diastolic dysfunction, volume status

I would discuss with surgeon regarding continuation vs stopping surgery if patient remains
unstable. Post-operative disposition to HD/ICU for monitoring. Cardiology referral for work-
up and optimisation when in HD/ICU
