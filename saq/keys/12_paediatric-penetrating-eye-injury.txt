Discuss your approach to the anaesthetic management of a screaming 5-year-old child with
penetrating eye injury.
(Modified from FHKCA 2009A, FANZCA 2015A)

Examiner Report
Sample Answer

This is an uncooperative pre-school patient who requires an urgent eye operation. He is likely in pain,
and may not be well-fasted. It is important to avoid situations and drugs which may increase intra-
ocular pressure (IOP).

Pre-op
• I will speak to surgeon regarding salvageability of the eye and urgency of the operation.
        o If eye is not salvageable, I will wait for adequate fasting time (6h from last meal) to
            minimise aspiration risk.
        o Analgesia should be provided in the mean time.
        o Prior to sending for the patient (to OT), I will request for ward staff to apply Ametop cream
            covered with a clear, adhesive dressing over bilateral hand dorsum.
• I will speak to patient and parents to get a targeted history to confirm that this is an isolated
    injury.
        o Mechanism of injury? Fasting time?
        o Any significant trauma? Concomitant injuries?
        o Other past medical history, including any drug allergy, recent URTI, family history of
            problems with general anaesthesia
• If patient is inconsolable during my assessment at the reception,
        o I will pre-medicate him with PO Midazolam 0.5mg/kg.
        o This will minimise patient’s distress (which could increase IOP), and facilitate patient’s
            cooperation during induction. This is balanced against aspiration risk.
        o I will also request for parental presence during induction, with distraction with
            toys/games/video (if the other eye is intact).

Intra-op
• Assuming child is sedated, with adequate time for Ametop cream application and IV cannulation,
    my preferred approach is that of IV induction (modified RSI) with micro-cuff ETT.
        o Pre-oxygenation with 100% O2, with parental presence, in warmed OT.
        o Induction with IV Propofol 2-3mg/kg, IV Rocuronium 1mg/kg, with standby IV
           Sugammadex 16mg/kg for reversal.
        o IV Lignocaine 1mg/kg and IV Fentanyl 2mg/kg to blunt cardiovascular response to
           laryngoscopy
        o Decompress stomach with OG tube after intubation
• I will take careful measures to avoid worsening IOP intra-op.
        o Maintain adequate depth of anaesthesia and muscle relaxation to avoid
           coughing/straining
        o Titrate ventilation settings to avoid hypoxia, hypercarbia.
        o Multi-modal analgesia to treat pain/ nociception
               ▪ Paracetamol and NSAIDS (if not already given in the ward/ED)
               ▪ Short acting opioids (Fentanyl)
               ▪ Topical LA/ RA by surgeon.
        o Avoid drugs which may increase IOP
               ▪ Nitrous Oxide
               ▪ Suxamethonium (controversial)
               ▪ Ketamine (controversial)
• I will monitor and prepare for oculo-cardiac and oculo-emetic reflexes intra-op.
        o IV Atropine 20mcg/kg drawn and ready for administration. Inform surgeon to stop if
           patient becomes severely bradycardic during manipulation.
        o Give anti-emetics IV Dexamethasone 0.1mg/kg and IV Ondansetron 0.15mg/kg.
•   I will extubate patient awake in a smooth and controlled manner.
         o Slow and gentle washing out of inhalational agent
         o Ensuring adequate analgesia before extubation
         o Anti-emetic prophylaxis (IV Dexa 0.1mg/kg and IV Ondansetron 0.15mg/kg)
         o IV Lignocaine 1mg/kg to blunt cardiovascular response during extubation
         o Minimize contact and noise stimulation in OT

Post-op
• I will recover patient in a quiet and dark area, with parental presence, in PACU.
        o This is to minimise emergence delirium, which patient is at higher risk of due to his age,
            significant distress pre-op, impaired vision (eye shield).
• To general ward if nil complications arising from surgery or anaesthesia.
• Regular analgesia with Paracetamol + anti-emetics as necessary.
