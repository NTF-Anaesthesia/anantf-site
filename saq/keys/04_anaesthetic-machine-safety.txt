4. Describe the safety features of anaesthetic workstation in preventing delivery of hypoxic mixture.

Sample Answer

1. High pressure system 137 Bar (Consists of Cylinder, 1st stage pressure regulator)
Cylinders
• Serves as O2 reserve to pipeline supply (with preferential usage of pipeline > cylinder)
• Colour-coded, labelled for easy identification and to avoid misconnections.
• Pin Index Safety System (PISS) to ensure correct O2 connection to anaesthesia machine
• Bodok seal to ensure tight connections, and prevent gas leak.
• Display of cylinder pressures to alert users to replace cylinders are exhausted

2. Intermediate pressure system 4 Bar (Consists of Pipeline, Fail-safe valve, O2 supply failure alarm,
     Oxygen flush valve, 2nd stage pressure regulator)
Pipeline
• Colour-coded, labelled for easy identification and to avoid misconnections.
• Diameter Index Safety System (DISS) and unique Schrader probe profile to ensure correct O2
     connection at wall socket
• Non-interchangeable Screw Thread (NIST) for hose connection to anaesthesia machine
• Display of pipeline pressures to alert users if pressures are low
Fail-safe valve/ Hypoxic mixture prevention device
• Cessation of N2O flow if no O2 flow is present
O2 supply failure alarm
• Generates an alarm and stops supply of other gases once O2 pressure falls below a predetermined
     critical level. (2 Bar) (Ritchie Whistle =>8s. New alarms continues until O2 pressure is restored)
Oxygen flush valve
• Delivers O2 at 35-70 L/min, 4 Bar to facilitate rapid administration of O2

3. Low pressure system 1 Bar (Flow control valve, flowmeters, vaporizers, check valve, common gas
    outlet and breathing circuit)
Flowmeter
• Large, fluted O2 knob, located first from left on rotameter for easy identification of O2 knob
• Minimum O2 flow rate of 250ml/min (Equivalent to O2 consumption of a resting adult)
• Anti-static material used for flowmeter to prevent stuck bobbin from impeding gas flows
• O2 being the last gas to enter the common gas outlet to avoid administration of other gases should
    there be upstream leakage/cracks.
• Low O2 delivery alarm (fuel cell analyser)
• Hypoxia Guard Proportioning system. Maintains ratio of O2:N2O at minimum ratio of 1:2
Breathing circuit
• Unidirectional valve to prevent mixture of expired gases (with lower FiO2)
• CO2 absorber to increase percentage of FiO2 by reducing percentage of FiCO2
• Reservoir bag to serve as FGF (and O2) reservoir
• Paramagnetic O2 analyser + Low O2 delivery alarm which monitors FiO2/ETO2 (via sampling tube
    from breathing circuit)
• Standardised 22mm/15mm common gas outlet diameters to minimize risk of wrong connections
Patient Monitor
• Low SpO2 alarm for warning of patient’s hypoxemia
