A. List the ways to assess a patient for the presence of residual neuromuscular blockade. (30%)
B. Discuss methods to reduce the risk of residual neuromuscular blockade (70%)

(A)
Clinically:
History
■ Dyspnea, muscle weakness
■ Review anaesthesia chart: last dose of NMBA, reversal dose (whether adequate for weight)
Examination
■ Poor respiratory effort: rapid shallow breathing, poor air entry, minimal chest movement, use of
accessory muscles of respiration
■■
■ Ptosis
■ Twitchy appearance
Unable to sustain head lift >/=5 seconds, weak hand grip Inability to swallow secretions
 • Investigations: desaturation, low pO2/raised pCO2 on ABG
Depth of neuromuscular blockade monitoring:
• Quantitative (train of four ratio <0.9): acceleromyography, electromyography, mechanomyography
• Qualitative: conventional peripheral nerve stimulator -- TOF count <4 with fade, double burst stimulation
with fade, post tetanic count low with fade
