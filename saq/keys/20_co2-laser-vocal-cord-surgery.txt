An otherwise healthy 45 year old man has a vocal cord nodule that the surgeon intends to
excise using a CO2 laser. He is a smoker but has no other significant medical history
A. Justify your anaesthetic technique of choice (50%).

My anaesthesia technique of choice is:

-   GA with TIVA Propofol.
-   Intubation with microlaryngeal tube (MLT) wrapped in laser-resistant material.
-   Positive pressure ventilation with NMBA to maintain paralysis.

Other possible techniques include:

1. THRIVE protocol using high flow nasal cannulation (HFNC)
2. High frequency jet ventilation (HFJV) via rigid bronchoscope.

-   These are tubeless techniques which have the advantages of improving surgical access.
-   Patient can be kept on spontaneous ventilation under MAC.
-   Airway topicalisation is necessary to facilitate airway instrumentation.

However, such tubeless techniques have downsides:

Increased risks of desaturation
- FiO2 is decreased during laser component of surgery
- Sedation is increased for patient’s comfort

Loss of airway
- Bleeding
- Tissue edema
- Laryngospasm (higher risk in a smoker)
- Secretions (higher risk in a smoker)

Airway trauma from laser/ surgical manipulation
- Which may be triggered inadvertent coughing/ movement by patient

Hence, my preference would be for GA with secured airway.

GA with TIVA Propofol.
- Allows for titration of depth of anaesthesia independent of gas flows (which will need to be
   adjusted during laser/ if patient desaturates)
- Less environmental/ air pollution compared to inhalational agents, especially if tubeless
   technique is used.

Intubation with MLT wrapped in laser-resistant material.
- Secured airway.
- Use of MLT improves surgical access to vocal cord nodule compared to normal sized laser
    ETT, but not as good as tubeless techniques.
- MLT has single cuff (vs double cuffs in laser ETT) which can be injected with blue dye
- Wrapping MLT in laser resistant material will reduce risk of airway fire, but not completely
    eliminate it.
- ETT patency is less affected by secretions/ bleeding.
- More accurate monitoring of gas flows and ETCO2 compared to tubeless techniques.
Positive pressure ventilation with NMBA to maintain paralysis.
- Better control of ventilation.
- Avoidance of inadvertent coughing, which increases risks of airway trauma compared to a
   patient who is not paralysed
- More responsive adjustment of FiO2 during laser phase, with ability to rapidly increase FiO2
   and gas flows if patient desaturates.

While I recognise the potential disadvantages of my chosen anaesthesia technique, which
include:

-   Risks of trauma to vocal nodule during intubation.
-   ETT is a potential fuel source in airway fire.
-   Poorer surgical access compared to tubeless techniques.

This technique is my preferred choice because it allows for a secured (shared) airway, better
control of ventilation/ oxygenation/ gas flows, and still provide surgeon with an immobile operating
field for CO2 laser of vocal cord nodule in a safe manner.
