You have been asked to see a previously well 64-year-old man in the PACU with SpO2 of 85% two
hours after laparoscopic right partial nephrectomy during which he lost 1 litre of blood. List the
likely causes of the desaturation and outline your approach to managing the patient’s hypoxaemia.
(From ANZCA 2014B)

Examiner Report
Sample Answer
(a)
The low SpO2 reading of 85% may be related to hypoventilation, V/Q mismatch, or poor peripheral
perfusion. Likely causes for his desaturations include:
• Anaesthesia-related
        o Atelectasis/ Mucus plugging
        o Laryngospasm triggered by secretions/ pain
        o Bronchospasm from delayed allergic reaction
        o Hypoventilation secondary to opioids, residual anaesthetics
        o Inadequate NMB reversal/ residual neuromuscular block
        o Upper airway obstruction from laryngeal oedema post-intubation
        o Negative pulmonary pressure oedema (if patient bit on ETT during extubation)
        o Pulmonary oedema from fluid overload (CCF/ renal failure post-nephrectomy).
        o TRALI/TACO if blood transfusion (esp FFP) has been administered
        o Aspiration pneumonitis
• Surgery-related
        o Blood loss leading to reduced O2 carrying capacity, severe anaemia interfering with pulse
             oximetry reading.
        o Subcutaneous emphysema/ pneumothorax
        o Splinting of diaphragm due to residual pneumoperitoneum
        o CO2 embolism from CO2 insufflation during pneumoperitoneum
• Patient-related
        o Exacerbation of underlying undiagnosed condition - patient was “previously well”
                 ▪ First presentation of asthma/ COPD exacerbation/ CCF/ neuromuscular disorders
                 ▪ Upper airway obstruction from OSA
                 ▪ Respiratory tract infection
        o Pulmonary embolism (pro-thrombotic state from underlying malignancy, compounded by
             long surgery)
        o CNS depression leading to hypoventilation
                 ▪ Stroke, post-operative cognitive dysfunction, hypoglycemia, hypothermia
        o Reduced cardiac output leading to hypoperfusion
                 ▪ Myocardial ischemia, heart failure, arrhythmias
• Other causes
        o Equipment error (artefactual due to cold peripheries/ shivering)

(b)
This is a concerning situation. I would call for help (alert senior, get trained assistants) and attend
immediately to this patient.

This patient has significant hypoxaemia 2h post-nephrectomy. I would simultaneously resuscitate
patient’s ABCs while assessing for possible causes of desaturation. I would also review the rest of
vitals (BP, HR, ECG) and confirm pletysmograph waveform to exclude erroneous readings.

Airway          Check and maintain airway patency with head-tilt chin lift manoeuvre, KIV nasal
                airway, KIV suctioning of upper airway secretions as required.
                If GCS <8, prepare for re-intubation.
Breathing       Give 100% FiO2 via NRM.
                Support with bag-mask ventilation followed by re-intubation if inadequate
                respiratory efforts/ poor oxygenation despite FiO2 100%.
Circulation     Feel for pulse. Look at ECG monitor for ischaemic changes and arrhythmias
                Run IV crystalloids fast via large bore IV cannula if patient is hypotensive
Evaluate causes for desaturations.
• Review history and anaesthesia notes
        o Any significant medical history (OSA/asthma/ COPD/ CCF/ neuromuscular disease/
            myopathy) or allergies?
        o Check anaesthesia charts for doses and timings for opioids, sedatives, drugs which patient
            is allergic to, neuromuscular blocking agents and reversal agents.
        o Check PCA pump for opioid doses administered.
• Physical examination
        o Auscultate chest to exclude tension pneumothorax (absent breath sound, tracheal
            deviation, hyperresonance to percussion) , anaphylaxis/ bronchospasm (wheeze),
            pulmonary oedema/ atelectasis (creps).
        o Palpate chest wall for subcutaneous emphysema.
        o Angioedema, rash (allergic/ blood transfusion reaction)
        o Check for surgical dressing and drain for evidence of ongoing bleeding.
        o Check TOF ratio (should be >0.9) using a nerve stimulator.
        o Assess respiratory rate, and check for pupillary size/ response to light (respiratory
            depression, constricted pupils in opioid overdose).
        o Assess GCS, and neurological status (stroke/ oversedation).
• POCT
        o Target POCUS to evaluate heart and lung pathologies.
        o ABG for respiratory status, and haemoglobin level (retroperitoneal blood loss).
        o Portable CXR (for pneumothorax, aspiration pneumonitis, pulmonary oedema)
        o 12-lead ECG if suspected myocardial ischaemia.
        o Capillary blood glucose, to check and correct hypoglycemia
• CT Brain if there is suspicion of stroke (GCS drop, focal neurological deficits).

Treatment should be directed towards underlying causes for desaturation.
• Encourage deep breathing to open up collapsed alveoli if patient is maintaining spontaneous
    ventilation.
• Maintain adequate oxygenation by providing supplementary O2. Bronchodilators for
    bronchospasm.
• Maintain normotension with fluids / vasopressors / inotropes. Inform surgeon for evaluation and
    transfuse PCT if ongoing bleeding is suspected.
• Treat residual anaesthesia drug effects with IV Naloxone titrated doses (0.04mg) for opioid
    overdose, IV Flumazenil for Benzodiazepine overdose, additional reversal agents if NMB was
    inadequately reversed.
• Intubate to secure airway and for control of ventilation if required. Lung recruitment manoeuvres
    and PEEP for treatment of atelectasis if patient is re-intubated.
• Needle decompression if tension pneumothorax, followed by chest tube insertion.

Documentation and critical incident reporting should be done up when patient is stable. Surgeon and
relatives should be informed. Patient should be sent to High Dependency Unit for monitoring, or ICU
for ventilatory support.
