Discuss fixed ratio blood product transfusion versus goal directed factor concentrate
administration in the management of a severely injured polytrauma patient.
From MMed 2021 (1)

Sample Answer

In the management of a severely injured polytrauma patient,

Fixed ratio blood product transfusion
• Refers to the empirical transfusion of FFP, Platelets, RBCs in 1:1:1 ratio
• Aims at early correction (with FFP and platelets) of trauma-induced coagulopathy
• Typically implemented as part of hospital’s Massive Transfusion Protocol (MTP)
• Standard of care for Damage Control Resuscitation (DCR) in trauma patients.

However, there are several issues surrounding this approach:
• Inadequate resuscitation in terms of factor replacement, especially fibrinogen, which is
  depleted earlier/ more in trauma-induced coagulopathy.
• Over-resuscitation in terms of volume load, leading to worsening haemorrhage.
• Delay in FFP administration (compared to RBC, platelets) due to thawing time
• Inconclusive evidence on ideal component ratio for transfusion (PROMMTT, PROPPR trial)
• Complications associated with blood product transfusion
      o Transfusion-associated circulatory overload (TACO)
      o Transfusion-associated acute lung injury (TRALI)
      o Transmission of infectious diseases

As such, there is a trend towards using goal directed factor concentrate-based management:
(i)    Goal-directed approach (instead of empirical administration)
       a. Using point-of-care visco-elastic measurement such as ROTEM, TEG with quick TAT
       b. Allows for more precise replacement of deficient components
       c. Reduces wastage of blood products
(ii)   Factor concentrate-based management (instead of FFP)
       a. Prothrombin complex concentrate (PCC) and Fibrinogen Concentrate (FC) can be
           made readily available in OT, avoiding need for long thawing time for FFP.
       b. Smaller administration volume, which facilitates permissive hypotension and
           minimises haemodilution
       c. No need for cross-matching. Avoids complications associated with blood product
           transfusion.

Given its potential benefits, medical institutions with established ROTEM/TEG-based
treatment algorithm, specialised equipment/drugs, and trained personnel should adopt a
goal directed factor concentrate-based management. Fixed ratio blood product transfusion
may still be useful during initial haemostatic resuscitation or if visco-elastic assays are normal.
