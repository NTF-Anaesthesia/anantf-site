The right ventricle

Is the RV big? Is it working?

Ask the two questions in order: first size and shape, then how well it contracts. A dilated, failing RV changes what you do next. A fluid bolus, induction or positive-pressure ventilation can tip it over.

Normal values follow the 2025 ASE right-heart guideline.

RV · Anatomy and physiology

The RV wraps around the LV.

In short axis the RV is a crescent wrapped around the round LV. In an apical view it is a triangle. When it dilates it becomes rounder and can take over the apex, which the LV normally forms.

It has two parts: an inflow (sinus) and an outflow (conus, or infundibulum). It contracts like a bellows, in a wave that runs from the inflow towards the outflow tract. The free wall runs from the apex to the outflow tract. Longitudinal shortening, inward free-wall movement and septal motion all help it eject.

Built for a low-pressure circuit
  • Pulmonary pressures are about one-sixth of systemic pressures, so the RV wall is thin.
  • Thin wall plus a low-resistance circuit means the RV is very sensitive to afterload. A sudden rise (PE, hypoxia, acidosis, high airway pressure) can make it dilate and fail. It copes with extra volume much better than with extra pressure.
  • It shares the septum with the LV. Normally the LV indents the RV, and LV contraction helps the RV eject. So LV function affects RV function, and the reverse.
Why this matters at induction

When the RV dilates, the septum moves towards the LV, LV filling falls and cardiac output falls. Low systemic pressure then reduces RV coronary perfusion, and the RV gets worse. Positive-pressure ventilation raises RV afterload. With a big, poorly contracting RV: support the blood pressure first, avoid reflex fluid boluses, and get senior help before you induce.

How to tell which ventricle is which

  • The tricuspid annulus sits slightly closer to the apex than the mitral annulus.
  • The moderator band crosses the RV near its apex.
  • The LV normally forms the apex.

Physiology statements are general teaching, not patient-specific advice.

Four windows. Find the RV in each.

Get an RV-focused apical view

  1. Obtain a standard A4C.
  2. Rock the probe slightly towards the RV so that it sits in the middle of the sector.
  3. Rotate until the RV is at its widest and you see the whole free wall and the tricuspid annulus. Keep the LV apex in view.
  4. Avoid clipping the lateral wall or cutting obliquely across the chamber: an off-axis plane undersizes the RV.
How a standard A4C can mislead

A view centred on the LV may show only part of the RV. Rotating the plane changes the apparent size and area. Use the same RV-focused plane, at comparable loading, for serial measurements.

RV · Morphology

Size first, then wall thickness.

Size, at end-diastole

Start with the eyeball. In an RV-focused A4C the normal RV is smaller than the LV and the LV forms the apex. If the RV is dilated, also look for an enlarged right atrium.

Free-wall thickness

  1. Use the subcostal view at end-diastole, in 2D or M-mode, at the level of the anterior tricuspid leaflet.
  2. Keep the beam perpendicular to the free wall.
  3. Exclude trabeculations, papillary muscles and epicardial fat.

A thick wall points to a chronic pressure load such as pulmonary hypertension. Restrictive and infiltrative cardiomyopathies also thicken it. Thickening cannot date the current deterioration or exclude acute-on-chronic disease: compare with old studies and the history.

The >5 mm threshold for hypertrophy matches current guidance. The 3 ± 1 mm normal and >10 mm severe grades are older values and were not re-checked against the 2025 guideline.

RV · Function

Eyeball first. FAC and TAPSE measure different things.

Start by watching the free wall: does it move in towards the septum, and does the tricuspid annulus move towards the apex? Then put numbers on it: FAC, then TAPSE. RV output (RVOT VTI) comes next, but it is beyond FoCUS and not taught here.

Fractional area change

In the same complete RV-focused A4C, trace the endocardial boundary at end-diastole and end-systole, including trabeculations within the cavity. Trace from the annulus along the free wall to the apex and back along the septum. Keep the whole contour visible.

FAC = (EDA − ESA) / EDA × 100%

FAC describes a change in projected area. It omits the RV outflow tract and is sensitive to tracing and plane. The 2025 ASE normal reference is >35%; FAC is not a direct measurement of RVEF.

TAPSE

Align the M-mode cursor with lateral tricuspid annular motion in an apical view. Measure the base-to-peak systolic excursion, rather than a chamber length or a tissue-Doppler velocity.

The 2025 ASE normal reference is >17 mm. TAPSE is angle- and load-dependent and mainly reflects basal longitudinal function. Translation and tethering can preserve excursion despite impaired global performance.

Why do older slides give a different TAPSE number?

The 2020 SCCM slides call TAPSE ≤16 mm highly suggestive of RV dysfunction on one slide and <16 mm on another. They also use FAC <35% for dysfunction. This lecture uses the 2025 ASE normal references (TAPSE >17 mm, FAC >35%). Interpret values near the cut-off with FAC, size, free-wall motion, septal shape and the clinical state.

Current references: ASE right-heart guideline 2025.

Real examples

RV · Septal interaction

When does the septum go flat?

In a true parasternal short axis at the papillary muscle level, the LV is round through the whole cycle. A loaded RV pushes the septum towards the LV and makes it a D. The timing tells you which kind of load.

Septum flat inSuggestsFor example
SystoleRV pressure overloadPE, pulmonary hypertension, ARDS or high airway pressure
Diastole onlyRV volume overloadSevere tricuspid regurgitation, atrial septal defect
Systole and diastoleCombined pressure and volume overloadPulmonary hypertension with significant TR

Severe pressure overload alone can keep the septum flat into early diastole, so judge the frame at end-systole carefully.

How to time it

  • Freeze and step frame by frame; use the ECG if you have one.
  • End-diastole: the frame at the QRS, as the mitral valve closes.
  • End-systole: the smallest LV cavity, just before the mitral valve opens.
  • Check the plane first. An oblique cut makes a normal LV look oval and fakes a D.

Practise the timing in the RV patterns lab: choose Chronic PH or RV volume overload, then freeze systole and diastole.

Question 1

What does this clip show?

Answer: RV segmental wall-motion abnormality consistent with RV infarction.

  • The right coronary artery supplies most of the RV, so RV infarction usually comes with inferior LV involvement. Here both the distal RV and the LV are affected.
  • The source notes call this a subcostal example, but the loop looks parasternal. Name the window from what you see, not from the label. A regional abnormality is different from a globally dilated, poorly moving RV.
  • These patients often depend on preload. Be careful with venodilators and positive-pressure ventilation.

Question 2

A 42-year-old woman with known pulmonary hypertension becomes shocked after aspiration in the operating room. What does this clip show?

Answer: dilated RV with moderate to severe dysfunction.

  • Aspiration and hypoxia raise pulmonary vascular resistance. A chronically loaded RV has little reserve.
  • She was treated with inhaled nitric oxide. Lowering pulmonary vascular resistance unloads the RV, the septum moves back and ventricular filling improves.

Answer: severely dilated RV and severe RV dysfunction.

  • The A4C shows thrombus at the RV apex along the free wall.
  • Lower-limb ultrasound showed DVT and a V/Q scan was high probability. He was thrombolysed and survived an acute PE.

The right ventricle

Describe it before diagnosing it.

  1. Acquire several windows and an RV-focused apical view.
  2. Report size, free-wall thickness, contraction and septal timing.
  3. Integrate FAC and TAPSE with the whole examination and loading conditions.
  4. RV dilatation or McConnell sign alone neither confirms nor excludes pulmonary embolism.
  5. Use the clinical context and serial change to decide what to investigate next.
