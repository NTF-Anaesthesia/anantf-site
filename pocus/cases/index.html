Case 1 · IVC technique

Hypotension in ICU. Is the IVC telling the truth?

1 · The patient

Hypotensive soon after ICU admission. The team wants the inferior vena cava (IVC) to guide volume status.

2 · The clip

3 · Commit: which of these is true?

4 · Reveal

  • The IVC moves in and out of the plane. A beam through the edge of a tube, not its centre, makes it look narrower: movement mimics collapse.
  • Fix the picture first: IVC in long axis, entering the right atrium, hepatic vein joining it. Rock and sweep to the widest diameter; hold that plane through the whole breath.
  • Measure 1–2 cm from the RA–IVC junction, at end-expiration (Lang 2015, ASE/EACVI).

What the right plane looks like

Teaching point

A wobbly IVC view is not a sin; treating a patient on one is. Check acquisition before physiology. Even a good IVC answers a narrow question: size and variation depend on breathing effort, ventilation and the right heart.

Case 2 · LV function

Failing to wean from NIV.

1 · The patient

74-year-old man, presumed chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), in respiratory distress on non-invasive ventilation (NIV). Fails several weaning attempts.

2 · Commit: what is the most appropriate next step?

Reveal

  • The COPD is only presumed. Repeated weaning failure: look for an unproven or unexcluded cause; the heart is the obvious one.
  • Removing positive pressure ↑ venous return and LV afterload. A ventricle that coped on NIV may fail off it → pulmonary oedema.
  • Repeating an unhelpful test, or waiting, changes nothing.

3 · The echo

4 · Commit: the cause of NIV failure is congestive heart failure. What is the ejection fraction?

Reveal

  • Severe global LV dysfunction in all three views: little thickening, cavity barely changes size. Three windows are enough to call EF severely reduced by eye.
  • No need for Simpson’s biplane here. Grade by eye in more than one view; measure when it would change management or to track change.
  • Outcome: negative fluid balance, weaned off NIV.

Advanced: diastolic Doppler indices in weaning (mitral inflow, tissue Doppler) are in Mayo 2016.

Teaching point

Keeps failing to come off positive pressure? Scan the heart before blaming the lungs. A poor LV makes weaning a cardiac stress test. Treat the cause (here, fluid off), then retry.

Case 3 · Masses and effusions

Lung cancer and shock on three vasopressors.

1 · The patient

Recently diagnosed lung cancer; shock on three vasopressors. Bedside echo.

2 · The clip

3 · Commit: what is the most likely diagnosis?

4 · Reveal

  • Large echogenic left atrial mass, plus pericardial fluid.
  • Outcome: thoracotomy showed tumour extending into the left atrium via the pulmonary veins.
  • Ultrasound describes; it gives no histology. Site, attachment, movement and story narrow the list. With a new cancer, think direct spread or metastasis as well as thrombus.

Teaching point

Find the finding, then ask how it explains the shock: effusion compressing a chamber? Mass blocking LV filling? Say what you see and what you cannot tell; get definitive imaging and the right team early.

Case 4 · RV against LV

Sepsis, a high CVP and low urine output.

1 · The patient

Sepsis, low urine output. Point-of-care echo.

  • HR 140/min
  • BP 135/65 mm Hg
  • CVP 18 mm Hg
  • Urine output low

2 · The clip

3 · Commit: which right ventricular finding does this show?

4 · Reveal

  • The RV looks vigorous because the LV beside it is hypokinetic. Read the ventricles against each other.
  • Not hypovolaemia: CVP 18 mm Hg and a poor LV do not fit an empty circulation.
  • Not McConnell sign: that is RV free-wall akinesia with a normally moving RV apex, a finding in the RV itself.
  • Outcome: aggressive fluids → respiratory distress. Work-up found left anterior descending (LAD) coronary disease.

Teaching point

Low urine output in sepsis is not an order for fluid. High CVP, poor LV, congested patient: the tank is not the problem. Before a bolus: needed? responsive? tolerated?

Case 5 · Regional wall motion

VF on day 1 after aortic root replacement.

1 · The patient

73-year-old man, aortic root replacement for severe chronic type A dissection. Ventricular fibrillation (VF) on postoperative day 1.

2 · The clips: before and after the arrest

Contrast (ultrasound enhancing agent, Optison) fills the LV cavity to show the endocardial border. Compare each wall before and after.

3 · Commit: what is the most appropriate next step?

4 · Reveal

  • After arrest: severe hypokinesis to akinesis of anterior wall, apex and septum; lateral (circumflex) and inferior (right coronary) walls mostly normal. LAD territory.
  • After VT/VF arrest, a segmental wall motion abnormality after return of circulation is a surrogate for coronary disease → refer for catheterisation (Levitov 2016).
  • Ventricular arrhythmia after cardiac surgery: think ischaemia. Root replacement reattaches the coronaries, so a new territorial abnormality needs a coronary answer.
  • Resternotomy, mechanical support or fluid treat other problems. The decision is the cardiac surgical and cardiology teams’; get your scan to them early.

Teaching point

Scan early after arrhythmia or arrest; compare with any baseline. A territorial wall motion abnormality changes the next step.

Case 6 · Source of sepsis

A septic 18-year-old. Where is the source?

1 · The patient

18-year-old man: sore throat, sweats, breathlessness, diarrhoea.

  • Temperature 40 °C
  • BP 87/42 mm Hg
  • Lactate 2.7 (units not stated)
2 · Commit: which ultrasound examination is most likely to reveal the source of his sepsis?

Reveal

All four were scanned. Look at each before reading the labels.

3 · The four-view survey

Answer: venous ultrasound

  • Diagnosis: Fusobacterium necrophorum bacteraemia with Lemierre syndrome, septic thrombophlebitis after a throat infection, classically of the internal jugular vein (view labelled only as right neck).
  • The sore throat was the clue. Heart normal, B-lines non-specific, abdomen clear: a survey works best when the history says where to look hardest.

Teaching point

Sepsis ultrasound is not only heart and lungs. Let the history pick the window. B-lines show lung fluid, not its cause.

Case 7 · Post-cardiac surgery

Hypotension after pacing-wire removal.

1 · The patient

67-year-old man after coronary artery bypass grafting; needed an intra-aortic balloon pump and open chest. Three weeks later, before ward transfer, epicardial pacing wires are removed and he becomes hypotensive.

2 · The clip

3 · Commit: what is the most appropriate next step?

4 · Reveal

  • Pericardial tamponade after pacing-wire removal: a localised haematoma compressing the RV.
  • After cardiac surgery, pericardial blood is often clotted and localised, not free and circumferential: a needle is unlikely to drain it. Re-exploration treats collection and bleeding.
  • Fluid and inotropes may buy minutes; they fix nothing.
  • Post-cardiac surgery TTE windows are often poor. A limited scan without a large effusion does not exclude tamponade; TOE if TTE is inadequate.

Teaching point

New hypotension after pacing-wire removal is tamponade until proven otherwise. Call the surgeon early; don’t wait for a textbook image.
